# ranunculus

Ranunculus is almost made for the scanner. Each bloom is built from many thin, tightly stacked petals, like the pages of a small book, and the light on a flatbed passes through those layers instead of bouncing off them. The result is a flower that looks lit from within, with a clear paper-thin edge and a dense, saturated core.

## how they scan

The layering is the whole story. A ranunculus can hold a hundred petals, and scanography records them without losing the ones underneath. You can see the spiral where the petals wind toward the center, and the way the color deepens ring by ring. Because the petals are so fine, the translucency comes through strongly: a white ranunculus is not flat white but a set of overlapping veils, and a coral or raspberry one carries its color all the way to the edge.

Ranunculus also scans cleanly because the blooms are relatively flat and round, so they sit close to the glass and stay sharp across the whole flower.

## commissioning a print of your ranunculus

Ranunculus is a common spring and early-summer wedding flower, and it scans best while fresh, before the petals loosen. If it was in your bouquet, reach out before your event to hold a commission slot, then ship the flowers after the day. The arrangement is scanned and composed, then printed on archival matte fine art paper in the size you choose.
