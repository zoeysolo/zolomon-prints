# garden roses

Garden roses are one of the best flowers to scan. Where a florist rose opens into a tight spiral, a garden rose keeps opening: dozens of soft, cupped petals that fold over each other and let light through at the edges. On a flatbed scanner, that structure reads beautifully. The petals nearest the glass stay crisp, and the ones behind them fall into shadow, so a single bloom shows real depth.

## how they scan

The detail people notice first is the color inside the petals. A garden rose is rarely one flat shade. It shifts from a pale, almost translucent edge to a denser tone at the center, and scanography holds that gradient at 1200 DPI without flattening it. The ruffled edges, which can look busy in a photograph, become the point of the image: you can follow a single petal from its base to its curl.

Cream, blush, and peach garden roses tend to glow against a black background. Deeper corals and reds hold their saturation and read as rich rather than dark.
